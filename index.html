Your phone finally has an agent of its own.

Atom is a free, open-source AI agent for Android. It reads the screen, taps and types through your apps, then checks the screen again before it says “done”. It runs real Linux tools, works on a schedule, and uses the model you pick, or none online at all.

  • Free and open source, GPL-3.0
  • Android 14+, ARM64
  • No account, no telemetry

Chat apps tell you how. Atom does it.

One app on the phone you already carry: hands for your apps, a workbench for real tools, and the context of your day, used only when you ask.

It uses your phone the way you do.

Atom opens apps, reads what’s on screen, taps, types and scrolls. Then it looks again to confirm the result. If it can’t confirm something worked, it tells you, instead of guessing.

Every step is listed in the chat as it happens, and Stop works at any point.

How app control works

One recorded run with a local 3B model. Its reply says what it hadn’t confirmed yet.

One tap away, over any app.

A bubble floats over whatever you’re doing. Tap it for a mini chat, hold it for quick actions, drag it anywhere. Or set Atom as the phone’s assistant and open it with a long press of the power button.

The bubble and the assistant

It knows your context, when you ask.

Notifications, one-time codes, calendar, contacts, files and location. Each sits behind its own Android permission, asked the first time a task needs it, with one sentence saying why. Say no and only that feature stays off.

Every permission, and when it’s asked

A real workbench in your pocket.

A terminal, plus Alpine or Debian Linux in PRoot with no root needed, or in a chroot if you have root. Git, curl, jq and ripgrep come with the base tools; Python and Node are added on request.

Scheduled tasks and phone-event automations run in the background and report back.

Linux on Android without root

Any model. Or no cloud at all.

14 providers built in, plus any OpenAI- or Anthropic-compatible endpoint, including a server on your own network. Sign in with a ChatGPT or Grok subscription instead of a key.

Or go fully offline: an optional 32 MB pack runs models on the phone with LiteRT-LM and llama.cpp.

Tested on real tasks.

Release candidates run these requests on an Android emulator in CI, against a small model (Qwen2.5 3B) on the runner’s own CPU. No cloud, no API key. The checks look at the phone, not at the wording.

Private by design.

No telemetry, no analytics, no account and no Atom server. Your phone talks to the services you choose, and nothing else.

Stays on your phone

  • Chats, memory, files, schedules and the Linux environment
  • API keys, encrypted with a key held in the Android Keystore
  • Notifications, messages, calendar and other context Atom reads

Leaves your phone

  • Your messages, to the model provider you pick. Nothing, with an on-device model.
  • Web searches, to the search engine you set: DuckDuckGo, Bing or Mojeek by default.
  • An update check to GitHub, only when you open About or tap Check for updates.
  • Shell commands ask first
  • Stop always works
  • Undo history for device changes
  • Locked means locked
  • A switch for every tool

Honest limits.

What Atom can’t do well yet, so you know before you install.

  • Small models are weak agents.On-device models handle chat and simple tool calls. For long, multi-step app tasks, use a strong cloud model.
  • App control goes through accessibility.It’s slower than your thumb, and apps that hide their screens from Android’s accessibility service can’t be operated.
  • PRoot is slower than native.Linux without root runs in PRoot, which costs speed, and some software won’t run there.
  • Tested on emulators.CI covers every change, but real-phone battery numbers aren’t measured yet. Tell us what you see.
  • English only.The interface is in English, on ARM64 phones with Android 14 or newer.
